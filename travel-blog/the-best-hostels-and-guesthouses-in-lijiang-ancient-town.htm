The Best Hostels and Guesthouses in Lijiang Ancient Town

  • Home
  • Travel Blog

Lijiang Ancient Town, a UNESCO World Heritage site nestled at the foot of the snow-capped Jade Dragon Snow Mountain in Yunnan Province, is one of China’s most enchanting destinations. With its cobblestone streets, intricate canals, and centuries-old Naxi architecture, this town has become a magnet for backpackers, digital nomads, and culture seekers alike. But let’s be real—finding the right place to stay can make or break your trip. You don’t just need a bed; you need a base that offers comfort, community, and a taste of local life. In this guide, I’ll walk you through the best hostels and guesthouses in Lijiang Ancient Town, from budget-friendly dormitories to charming boutique stays that feel like home. Whether you’re here to party, meditate, or explore the surrounding Tiger Leaping Gorge, these accommodations will elevate your journey.

Why Lijiang Ancient Town is a Hostel and Guesthouse Paradise

Before diving into the specifics, let’s talk about why Lijiang is such a hotspot for travelers seeking unique lodging. The ancient town is a labyrinth of narrow alleys, each lined with traditional wooden buildings that have been converted into cozy hostels, guesthouses, and cafes. The vibe here is laid-back yet vibrant, with a mix of local Naxi culture and international backpacker energy. Many accommodations are family-run, offering personalized service that you won’t find in a chain hotel. Plus, the town’s layout means you’re never far from a bustling square, a quiet temple, or a riverside bar. But with so many options, how do you choose? Let’s break it down by neighborhood and style.

The Old Town Core: Where History Meets Hustle

If you want to be in the thick of it, staying in the heart of Lijiang Ancient Town is your best bet. This area is packed with shops, restaurants, and nightlife, but it can also get noisy. For budget travelers, hostels here offer unbeatable access to attractions like the Mu Family Mansion and Sifang Street.

Lijiang Old Town International Youth Hostel

This is a classic choice for backpackers who want a social atmosphere without sacrificing cleanliness. Located just a five-minute walk from Sifang Street, this hostel is housed in a traditional Naxi courtyard with red lanterns and wooden beams. The dorms are basic but spotless, with sturdy bunk beds and personal lockers. What sets it apart is the common area—a sprawling courtyard with hammocks, tables, and a small bar where travelers gather for nightly games of cards or beer pong. The staff organizes group hikes to Jade Dragon Snow Mountain and bike tours to nearby Shuhe Ancient Town. Prices start at around $8 for a dorm bed, and private rooms are available for $25. The only downside? The wifi can be spotty during peak hours, but that’s a small price to pay for the location.

Naxi Family Garden Guesthouse

For those who want a more intimate experience, this guesthouse is a gem. It’s a small, family-run property tucked away on a quiet lane off the main tourist drag. The owner, Auntie Li, is a Naxi elder who greets every guest with a cup of pu-erh tea and a warm smile. The rooms are decorated with traditional embroidery and antique furniture, and some have private balconies overlooking the canal. The best part? Auntie Li offers cooking classes where you can learn to make local dishes like crossing-the-bridge noodles and yak butter tea. Double rooms start at $40, which is a steal for the authenticity you get. Just be prepared for early mornings—Auntie Li likes to start her day at 6 a.m. with a sweeping session that might wake you up.

The Southern Section: Quieter, Cheaper, and More Local

If the hustle of the old town center feels overwhelming, head south. This part of Lijiang is less touristy, with more residential streets and fewer souvenir shops. It’s also closer to the bus station, making it convenient for day trips.

Yak’s Backpackers Hostel

Don’t let the name fool you—this hostel is anything but rough. Yak’s Backpackers is a hip, eco-friendly spot that attracts a younger crowd. The building is a restored Naxi house with solar panels and a rainwater collection system. The dorms are compact but cleverly designed, with privacy curtains and reading lights. The real highlight is the rooftop terrace, which offers panoramic views of the ancient town and the snow-capped mountains in the distance. Every evening, the hostel hosts a free BBQ where guests can grill skewers of lamb and vegetables while swapping travel stories. Dorm beds are $10, and private rooms are $30. The only catch is that the hostel is a 15-minute walk from the main attractions, but the tranquility is worth it.

Lijiang Slow Life Guesthouse

As the name suggests, this guesthouse is all about relaxation. It’s run by a young couple who moved to Lijiang from Shanghai, trading corporate jobs for a slower pace of life. The property has a small garden with a koi pond, a library filled with travel books, and a common kitchen where guests can cook their own meals. The rooms are minimalist but comfortable, with floor-to-ceiling windows that let in natural light. The owners are happy to recommend off-the-beaten-path spots, like a hidden waterfall in the nearby mountains or a local market where you can buy fresh mushrooms. Prices are reasonable at $35 for a double room, and they offer discounts for longer stays.

The Waterfront Area: Romance and R&R

Lijiang’s canals are its lifeblood, and staying near the water is a dream for photographers and romantics. This area is dotted with guesthouses that have direct access to the waterways, complete with weeping willows and stone bridges.

Canal View Boutique Hostel

This is a hybrid between a hostel and a boutique hotel, offering both dorms and private suites. The property is situated right on the main canal, with a terrace that extends over the water. You can sit there with a cup of coffee and watch the ducks paddle by. The dorms are spacious, with high ceilings and exposed brick walls, while the private rooms feature king-sized beds and rain showers. The hostel also has a small spa offering foot massages after a long day of walking. Prices range from $12 for a dorm to $60 for a deluxe suite. The only issue is that the canal can get a bit smelly in the summer, so pack some nose plugs if you’re sensitive.

Lijiang Waterfront Guesthouse

For a more upscale experience, this guesthouse is a top pick. It’s a restored mansion that once belonged to a Naxi merchant, complete with intricate wood carvings and a tranquil courtyard. Each room is uniquely decorated, with themes like “Bamboo Forest” or “Tea Ceremony.” The guesthouse offers a complimentary breakfast of local pastries and fruit, and the staff can arrange private tours to the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain or the Lashihai Lake. The best room is the “Silk Road Suite,” which has a four-poster bed and a private garden. Prices start at $80 per night, but if you’re splitting with a partner or friend, it’s worth the splurge.

Beyond the Ancient Town: Day Trips and Nearby Stays

While staying inside the ancient town is convenient, don’t limit yourself. The surrounding areas offer unique experiences that are worth a short bus ride or taxi.

Shuhe Ancient Town: The Quiet Cousin

Just 20 minutes north of Lijiang, Shuhe is a smaller, more serene version of the ancient town. It’s less crowded and more affordable, making it a great base for travelers who want peace and quiet. The Shuhe Lotus Hostel is a standout here, with a beautiful garden and a yoga deck. Dorm beds are $7, and private rooms are $20. The hostel also rents bicycles for $5 a day, so you can explore the countryside at your own pace.

Baisha Village: The Artisan Hub

Baisha is famous for its mural paintings and traditional crafts. The Baisha Art Hostel is a creative space where guests can take painting classes or learn pottery. The owners are local artists who display their work in the common area. Dorm beds are $9, and private rooms are $25. The hostel also has a rooftop bar that serves homemade plum wine.

Practical Tips for Booking in Lijiang

Now that you know where to stay, here are some pro tips to make your booking smooth:

  • Book in advance during peak season: Lijiang is busiest from April to October, especially during Chinese holidays like National Day (October 1-7) and May Day (May 1-5). Hostels and guesthouses fill up fast, so reserve at least two weeks ahead.
  • Check for hidden fees: Some places charge extra for heating or air conditioning, especially in winter. Ask before you book.
  • Bring earplugs: The ancient town can be noisy, with roosters crowing at dawn and bar music playing until late. Earplugs are a lifesaver.
  • Use local booking platforms: While Booking.com and Hostelworld work fine, Chinese platforms like Ctrip or Qunar often have better deals. You might need a local phone number to register, but it’s worth the effort.
  • Negotiate for long stays: If you’re staying for a week or more, many guesthouses offer discounts. Don’t be afraid to ask.

The Social Scene: Hostels, Bars, and Nightlife

Lijiang’s hostel culture is legendary for its social vibe. Many hostels have their own bars or partner with nearby pubs for happy hour deals. The Lijiang Old Town International Youth Hostel has a bar that features live music on weekends, while Yak’s Backpackers organizes pub crawls to local spots like the “Black Bear Bar” and “Tibetan Sky.” If you prefer a quieter evening, head to Naxi Family Garden Guesthouse for a tea ceremony with Auntie Li. She’ll tell you stories about Naxi folklore while you sip on oolong tea.

Food and Drink: What to Eat Near Your Stay

Lijiang is a food lover’s paradise, and your accommodation’s location will determine your culinary options. Near the old town core, you’ll find everything from street stalls selling “Lijiang Baba” (a fried flatbread) to upscale restaurants serving Yunnan-style hotpot. The Canal View Boutique Hostel is right next to a famous noodle shop that serves “Guoqiao Mixian” (crossing-the-bridge noodles) for $3. In the southern section, Yak’s Backpackers is near a night market where you can try grilled fish and spicy skewers. For a splurge, the Lijiang Waterfront Guesthouse has a restaurant that offers a tasting menu of Naxi dishes for $20 per person.

Sustainable Travel: How Your Stay Can Help the Community

Many hostels and guesthouses in Lijiang are committed to sustainable tourism. The Yak’s Backpackers uses solar energy and composts food waste, while the Naxi Family Garden Guesthouse sources ingredients from local farmers. By choosing these accommodations, you’re supporting the local economy and preserving Naxi culture. Some places even offer volunteer opportunities, like teaching English at a nearby school or helping with conservation projects in the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain area.

Final Thoughts on Lijiang’s Hostel and Guesthouse Scene

Lijiang Ancient Town is more than just a destination—it’s a feeling. Whether you’re sipping tea in a Naxi courtyard, chatting with fellow travelers on a hostel rooftop, or exploring the canals by moonlight, the right accommodation can transform your trip. From the budget-friendly dorms of Lijiang Old Town International Youth Hostel to the luxurious rooms of Lijiang Waterfront Guesthouse, there’s something for every traveler. So pack your bags, book your stay, and get ready for an adventure that blends history, nature, and community. Lijiang is waiting, and your perfect home away from home is just a click away.
