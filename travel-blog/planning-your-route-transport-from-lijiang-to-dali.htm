Planning Your Route: Transport from Lijiang to Dali

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

  • Home
  • Travel Blog

If you are mapping out a Yunnan adventure, the corridor between Lijiang and Dali is one of the most scenic and culturally rich stretches you will ever traverse. These two ancient cities, both UNESCO World Heritage contenders in their own right, sit roughly 160 kilometers apart by road, but the journey between them is far more than a simple transfer. It is a ribbon of mountain vistas, lakeside panoramas, and ethnic Bai and Naxi heritage. Whether you are a budget backpacker, a luxury-seeking traveler, or a digital nomad hunting for the next Instagrammable moment, the way you choose to travel from Lijiang to Dali will shape your entire Yunnan experience. Let us dive deep into every option, every hidden tip, and every scenic detour that makes this route a travel hotspot in its own right.

The Classic High-Speed Rail: Speed Meets Scenery

Why the Train Wins for Most Travelers

The high-speed rail connecting Lijiang to Dali is arguably the most popular choice among modern tourists, and for good reason. The Dali-Lijiang Railway, part of China’s sprawling high-speed network, cuts travel time down to roughly two hours. That is a massive improvement over the old overnight sleeper trains that once took nearly six hours. The trains are clean, punctual, and equipped with air conditioning, comfortable seats, and even USB charging ports. For the traveler who values efficiency without sacrificing the romance of the journey, this is the gold standard.

Booking Your Ticket: A Step-by-Step

Tickets can be purchased through the official China Railway app (12306), third-party platforms like Trip.com or Ctrip, or directly at the Lijiang Railway Station. During peak seasons—Chinese National Day (October 1st week), Spring Festival (January/February), and summer holidays (July-August)—tickets sell out fast. Book at least three to five days in advance. Second-class seats (二等座) cost around 80 to 100 RMB, while first-class seats (一等座) run about 130 to 160 RMB. First-class offers wider seats and more legroom, but honestly, the two-hour ride is short enough that second-class is perfectly comfortable.

The Scenic Highlights from the Window

Do not nap through this ride. The train snakes through the Erhai Lake basin, offering fleeting but stunning glimpses of the lake’s turquoise waters framed by the Cangshan Mountains. As you approach Dali, the landscape shifts from dry, rocky hills to lush, terraced farmland dotted with traditional Bai-style white houses with black-tiled roofs. Keep your camera ready for the moment the train emerges from a tunnel and Dali Old Town appears in the distance, with the Three Pagodas of Chongsheng Temple standing like ancient sentinels.

Pro Tips for Train Travelers

  • Arrive early at Lijiang Station. The station is about 20 minutes from Lijiang Old Town by taxi (around 30 RMB). Security checks can be slow during holidays.
  • Pack snacks and water. While there is a dining car on some trains, the selection is limited and overpriced. Local dried beef and flower cakes (鲜花饼) make excellent travel snacks.
  • Choose a window seat (A or F). On most CRH trains, seats labeled A and F are window seats. For the best views, sit on the left side of the train heading south toward Dali.

The Scenic Bus Ride: Budget-Friendly and Flexible

The Long-Distance Bus Option

If you are traveling on a shoestring budget or simply prefer ground-level views, the bus from Lijiang to Dali is a viable alternative. Buses depart from Lijiang’s main bus station (丽江客运站) and arrive at Dali’s bus station near the new town of Xiaguan. The journey takes about three to three and a half hours, depending on traffic and road conditions. Tickets cost between 50 and 70 RMB, making it the cheapest option by far.

What to Expect on the Road

The bus route follows the G5611 expressway, which winds through valleys and over mountain passes. The scenery is more intimate than the train: you will see roadside vendors selling local fruits, small villages with their own market days, and farmers leading water buffalo along the shoulder of the road. However, the ride can be bumpy in places, and the bus may make one or two rest stops at roadside convenience stores. These stops are great opportunities to stretch your legs and buy a stick of grilled tofu or a bag of freshly picked cherries.

Bus vs. Train: A Quick Comparison

| Aspect | High-Speed Train | Long-Distance Bus | |--------|------------------|-------------------| | Travel Time | 2 hours | 3-3.5 hours | | Cost | 80-160 RMB | 50-70 RMB | | Comfort | High, smooth ride | Moderate, bumpy in spots | | Scenery | Distant panoramic views | Ground-level, village views | | Flexibility | Fixed schedule | Multiple departures hourly |

When the Bus Makes Sense

  • You are heading to a specific village near Dali. The bus station in Xiaguan is closer to rural areas like Xizhou or Shaxi (if you are continuing west).
  • You have heavy luggage. Buses have underfloor storage, while trains require you to haul bags up narrow aisles.
  • You want to avoid the train station crowds. During Golden Week, the train station can be a zoo. The bus station is often less chaotic.

The Private Car or Didi: Freedom on the Road

Hiring a Driver for the Day

For travelers who crave flexibility and comfort, hiring a private car or using a ride-hailing app like Didi Chuxing is the ultimate luxury. A private transfer from Lijiang to Dali costs between 300 and 500 RMB, depending on the vehicle type and the driver’s reputation. You can book through your hotel, a local travel agency, or directly via Didi’s intercity ride option.

The Freedom to Detour

This is where the private car truly shines. Instead of taking the direct expressway, you can ask your driver to take the old G214 national road, which winds through the Heqing Valley and passes the stunning Shaxi Ancient Town. Shaxi is a hidden gem—a restored Bai trading post on the ancient Tea Horse Road that sees far fewer tourists than Lijiang or Dali. You can stop for a bowl of crossing-the-bridge noodles (过桥米线) in a local eatery, photograph the weathered wooden bridges, and browse the weekly market without a crowd.

Navigating Didi in Yunnan

Didi is widely available in both Lijiang and Dali, but intercity rides require some planning. Open the app, select “Intercity” (跨城), and enter Lijiang as your pickup and Dali as your destination. The app will show you a price range and estimated time. Drivers may ask for a small additional fee if you want to stop at scenic points along the way—negotiate this upfront. Also, note that Didi drivers in Yunnan are generally friendly and may offer to act as an informal guide, pointing out local landmarks and recommending photo spots.

Safety and Comfort Tips

  • Share your route with a friend. Use WeChat’s live location feature.
  • Carry cash. Some drivers prefer cash payment, especially if you negotiate a price outside the app.
  • Agree on stops before departure. If you want to visit Shaxi or Erhai Lake viewpoints, make sure your driver knows the plan.

The Adventurous Scooter or Motorcycle Rental

For the Free-Spirited Traveler

Yunnan’s roads are a paradise for motorcycle enthusiasts. Renting a scooter (usually 125cc to 150cc) in Lijiang and riding to Dali is an increasingly popular choice among younger travelers and digital nomads. Rental shops in Lijiang Old Town charge around 80 to 150 RMB per day, with discounts for multi-day rentals. You will need an International Driving Permit (IDP) or a Chinese driver’s license—police checks are common on the main roads.

The Route: G214 via Shaxi

The most scenic motorcycle route follows the old G214 highway, which parallels the expressway but hugs the mountains more closely. This road takes you through pine forests, past terraced rice paddies, and over the Jianchuan Pass, where the air is crisp and the views stretch for miles. The total distance is about 180 kilometers, and with stops, the ride takes four to five hours.

What You Need to Know Before You Go

  • Fuel stations are sparse. Fill up in Lijiang before you leave. There is a station in Heqing town, about halfway, but do not rely on it.
  • Weather changes fast. A sunny morning can turn into a chilly, rainy afternoon. Pack a waterproof jacket and gloves.
  • Road conditions vary. The G214 is generally well-paved, but some sections near Shaxi have loose gravel and potholes. Take it slow.
  • Altitude affects performance. At elevations around 2,400 meters (7,874 feet), your scooter’s engine may feel sluggish. Do not push it.

Why This Is a Travel Hotspot Trend

Riding a scooter through Yunnan has become a rite of passage for adventure travelers. Social media is flooded with videos of riders cruising past golden rice fields with the Cangshan Mountains as a backdrop. It is not just transport; it is an experience. You can stop at random villages, chat with local Bai farmers, and eat lunch at a roadside noodle stall that has no English menu. This is the kind of travel that creates stories, not just photos.

The Luxury Option: Chartered Minivan with a Guide

When Comfort Meets Culture

For families, groups of friends, or travelers over 50 who prefer a curated experience, chartering a minivan with an English-speaking guide is the premium choice. Prices range from 800 to 1,500 RMB per day, depending on the vehicle and guide’s qualifications. This option includes door-to-door service, flexible timing, and insider knowledge that no guidebook can provide.

The Itinerary Your Guide Might Suggest

A good guide will not just drive you from point A to point B. They will design a route that maximizes your cultural immersion. Here is a sample itinerary:

  • 9:00 AM – Pick up from your Lijiang hotel.
  • 9:30 AM – Stop at the Lashi Lake viewpoint for photos of the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain reflected in the water.
  • 11:00 AM – Visit a traditional Bai tie-dye workshop in Zhoucheng village. Watch artisans create intricate blue-and-white patterns.
  • 12:30 PM – Lunch at a local farmhouse in Xizhou, where you can try “sour and spicy fish” (酸辣鱼) fresh from Erhai Lake.
  • 2:00 PM – Arrive at Dali Old Town. Your guide will walk you through the winding alleys, pointing out the best coffee shops and hidden temples.
  • 4:00 PM – Check into your hotel, with the guide ensuring your reservation is correct.

Who Should Choose This Option

  • First-time visitors to China who may feel overwhelmed by language barriers.
  • Senior travelers who prefer not to handle luggage or navigate public transport.
  • Photography enthusiasts who want to stop at specific locations for golden hour shots.

The Slow Travel Option: Cycling from Lijiang to Dali

A Multi-Day Adventure

For the truly dedicated, cycling from Lijiang to Dali is a bucket-list journey. The distance is manageable over two to three days, with overnight stops in Shaxi or Jianchuan. You can rent a decent mountain bike in Lijiang for about 60 to 100 RMB per day. The route is mostly flat along the valley floor, with one significant climb near the Jianchuan Pass.

Why Cyclists Are Flocking to This Route

The Lijiang-Dali cycling route has gained traction on platforms like Komoot and Strava. It offers a rare combination of cultural sites, natural beauty, and manageable distances. You will pass through villages that see few tourists, where children wave and shout “Hello!” as you pedal by. The road is quiet on weekdays, with only the occasional bus or truck. Plus, the endorphin rush of arriving in Dali under your own power is unmatched.

Practical Tips for Cyclists

  • Start early. Leave Lijiang by 7:00 AM to avoid afternoon heat.
  • Carry plenty of water. There are long stretches between villages.
  • Use a GPS app. Maps.me or Gaode Maps work well offline.
  • Book accommodation in advance. Shaxi has several guesthouses popular with cyclists, but they fill up fast during holidays.

The Emerging Trend: Electric Vehicle (EV) Road Trips

Yunnan’s EV Infrastructure

Yunnan Province has been aggressively building out its electric vehicle charging network. As of 2025, there are fast-charging stations in Lijiang, Dali, and along the expressway. Renting an EV from a company like Tesla, NIO, or BYD is becoming a trendy option for eco-conscious travelers. Rental prices start at around 300 RMB per day for a compact EV.

The Silent, Smooth Ride

Driving an EV on the Lijiang-Dali route is a revelation. The car is whisper-quiet, so you can hear the birds and the wind. The instant torque makes climbing mountain passes effortless. And there is a certain cachet to arriving in Dali in a sleek electric car, knowing you left zero emissions behind.

Charging Logistics

  • Lijiang: Charging stations are available at the Lijiang Railway Station parking lot and several hotels.
  • En route: The expressway service area near Heqing has four fast chargers.
  • Dali: Dali Old Town has a dedicated charging hub near the south gate.

Plan your stops carefully. The total range of most EVs is around 300 to 400 kilometers, so you should have plenty of battery left, but it is always wise to top up at the halfway point.

Hidden Gems Along the Way

Shaxi Ancient Town: The Tea Horse Road’s Best-Kept Secret

Shaxi deserves its own paragraph. This restored Bai town was a major stop on the ancient Tea Horse Road, where caravans of horses carried tea from Yunnan to Tibet and beyond. Today, it is a sleepy, beautiful town with cobblestone streets, a weekly market every Friday, and the stunning Xingjiao Temple, which dates back to the Ming Dynasty. Most tourists skip Shaxi because it is not on the main train line, but that is precisely its charm.

Erhai Lake Viewpoints

If you are driving or cycling, make a detour to the eastern shore of Erhai Lake. The road between Wase and Shuanglang offers some of the most photographed views in China. Stop at a lakeside café, order a cup of Yunnan coffee, and watch the fishermen cast their nets against the backdrop of the Cangshan Mountains.

The Three Pagodas of Dali

Even if you have seen them in a thousand Instagram posts, the Three Pagodas of Chongsheng Temple are worth a visit. They are the symbol of Dali, standing since the 9th century. The reflection pool in front of the pagodas is the classic photo spot, but go early in the morning to avoid the crowds and catch the soft light.

Final Practical Advice for Your Journey

Packing for the Yunnan Climate

Yunnan’s weather is famously unpredictable. In summer (June to August), temperatures range from 18°C to 28°C (64°F to 82°F), but afternoon thunderstorms are common. In winter (December to February), it can drop to 2°C (35°F) at night. Pack layers: a lightweight fleece, a waterproof jacket, comfortable walking shoes, and a sun hat. The UV index is high at this altitude, so sunscreen is non-negotiable.

Language and Communication

English is not widely spoken outside of tourist areas. Download a translation app like Google Translate or Pleco. Learn a few key phrases: “Xie xie” (thank you), “Duo shao qian?” (how much?), and “Zhe ge, na ge” (this one, that one). Most importantly, have your destination written in Chinese characters to show taxi drivers.

Connectivity

China requires a VPN for access to Google, Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp. Install a reliable VPN before you leave. Alternatively, use local apps like WeChat for messaging and Alipay for payments. Most hotels and cafés in Lijiang and Dali offer free Wi-Fi.

Health and Safety

Altitude sickness is rare at 2,400 meters, but some people do feel lightheaded. Drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol on your first day, and eat light meals. The food in Yunnan is generally safe, but stick to busy restaurants with high turnover for the freshest ingredients. Street food is delicious but choose stalls where you can see the food being cooked.

The Verdict: Which Transport Option Is Right for You?

There is no single best way to travel from Lijiang to Dali. The high-speed train wins for speed and reliability. The private car wins for flexibility and the ability to explore hidden gems like Shaxi. The scooter wins for adventure and Instagram-worthy spontaneity. The bus wins for budget travelers. And the chartered minivan wins for those who want a stress-free, culturally rich experience.

The beauty of this route is that it is not just a transfer; it is a destination in itself. Whether you are gazing at Erhai Lake from a train window, tasting grilled tofu at a roadside bus stop, or feeling the wind in your hair on a scooter, the journey from Lijiang to Dali is a microcosm of everything that makes Yunnan magical. Plan your route, but leave room for detours. The best memories are often the ones you did not plan at all.
