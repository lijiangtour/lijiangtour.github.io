The Best Lijiang Photography Spots in Summer

Lijiang, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Yunnan Province, transforms into a photographer’s paradise during the summer months. While many travelers associate this ancient city with misty mornings and snow-capped peaks in winter, summer brings a different kind of magic—vibrant greenery, dramatic monsoon clouds, and golden sunsets that paint the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain in hues of orange and pink. The rainy season, from June to August, might deter some, but for photographers, it offers soft, diffused light and fewer crowds. Here is a curated guide to the best Lijiang photography spots in summer, blending iconic landmarks with lesser-known gems.

Old Town of Lijiang: The Timeless Backdrop

Sifang Street (Square Street)

The heart of Lijiang’s Old Town, Sifang Street, is a bustling square surrounded by ancient Naxi architecture. In summer, the cobblestones glisten after a light rain, reflecting the colorful umbrellas of tourists and the red lanterns hanging from wooden eaves. Arrive at dawn—around 6:00 AM—to capture the square empty of crowds. The soft morning light filters through the mist, creating a dreamy atmosphere. Use a wide-angle lens to emphasize the geometric patterns of the stone pathways and the layered rooftops. For a unique angle, climb to the second floor of a nearby tea house; the elevated view frames the square against the distant mountains.

Wangu Tower

Perched on Lion Hill, Wangu Tower offers a panoramic view of the entire Old Town. Summer afternoons can be hazy, but the golden hour before sunset is spectacular. The tower itself is a classic Naxi-style structure with intricate carvings, and the surrounding gardens are lush with blooming jasmine and bougainvillea. Photograph the tower from the base using a low angle to include the sky, or climb to the top for a sweeping vista. The best shot is during the “blue hour” just after sunset, when the city lights begin to twinkle against the deep indigo sky. A tripod is essential here for long exposures.

The Water Wheels

Located at the entrance of the Old Town, the two giant water wheels are iconic symbols of Lijiang. In summer, the water in the creek is crystal clear, and the wheels turn slowly, creating a soothing rhythm. Capture them during a light drizzle—the wet wood and stone take on a rich, saturated look. Use a slow shutter speed (1/15 second) to blur the water slightly, adding a sense of motion. Position yourself behind the wheels to include the Black Dragon Pool in the background, where the reflection of the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain appears on calm days.

Black Dragon Pool Park: The Classic Reflection Shot

The Moon Embracing Pavilion

This is, without a doubt, the most photographed spot in Lijiang. The Moon Embracing Pavilion, a small white structure with a curved roof, sits at the edge of the Black Dragon Pool. In summer, the pool is surrounded by weeping willows and blooming water lilies. The classic shot is taken from the east side of the pool, with the pavilion in the foreground and the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain perfectly mirrored in the water. The best time is early morning, around 7:00 AM, when the wind is calm and the surface is like glass. Use a polarizing filter to reduce glare and enhance the reflection. If clouds obscure the mountain, wait—summer storms often clear quickly, revealing the peak in dramatic light.

The Nine-Bend Bridge

A short walk from the pavilion, the Nine-Bend Bridge zigzags across the pool. In summer, the bridge is framed by lotus leaves and pink blooms. Photograph it from the shore using a telephoto lens to compress the perspective, making the bridge appear longer. Alternatively, stand on the bridge and shoot downward to capture the ripples and the fish swimming beneath. The bridge is also a great spot for candid shots of locals practicing tai chi or playing traditional instruments under the shade of ancient trees.

Jade Dragon Snow Mountain: Summer’s Hidden Beauty

Blue Moon Valley

While the mountain’s glaciers are less visible in summer due to frequent cloud cover, the lower slopes are bursting with color. Blue Moon Valley, named for its turquoise lakes, is a must-visit. The water gets its vibrant hue from minerals and glacial silt. In summer, the surrounding meadows are carpeted with wildflowers—yellow rhododendrons, purple asters, and red azaleas. Walk along the wooden boardwalks to find the best angles. Use a wide aperture (f/2.8) to isolate a single flower against the blurred blue water, or go wide to capture the entire valley with the mountain as a backdrop. The light is best between 10:00 AM and 12:00 PM, when the sun is high enough to illuminate the water without harsh shadows.

Yak Meadow

At an altitude of 3,500 meters, Yak Meadow offers a high-altitude grassland experience. In summer, the meadow is dotted with yaks grazing peacefully, and the air is crisp and cool. The clouds often hang low, creating a mystical atmosphere. Photograph the yaks from a distance with a telephoto lens to avoid disturbing them. For a landscape shot, include a single yak in the foreground to give scale to the vast meadow and the mountain behind. The light here changes rapidly—one minute it’s bright and sunny, the next it’s overcast with dramatic shafts of light breaking through the clouds. Be patient and wait for those moments.

Lashi Lake: A Summer Wetland Wonderland

The Bird Watching Platform

Lashi Lake, about 30 minutes from Lijiang, is a protected wetland that comes alive in summer. Migratory birds such as black-necked cranes and herons arrive in June and stay until August. The bird watching platform on the east side of the lake provides an elevated view. Use a 200-400mm lens to capture birds in flight against the backdrop of the lake and the distant mountains. The best time is early morning when the birds are most active. The soft, warm light of sunrise also adds a golden glow to the reeds and water.

The Floating Boardwalk

A newer addition to Lashi Lake is a wooden boardwalk that extends into the wetland. In summer, the boardwalk is flanked by tall reeds and water lilies. Photograph it from the shore using a long exposure to blur the clouds moving overhead, creating a sense of tranquility. Alternatively, walk to the end of the boardwalk and shoot back toward the shore, where the Naxi-style houses are reflected in the still water. This spot is particularly photogenic during a summer storm, when the dark clouds contrast with the bright green reeds.

Shuhe Old Town: The Quieter Neighbor

The Tea Horse Road

Shuhe, a quieter alternative to Lijiang’s Old Town, was a key stop on the ancient Tea Horse Road. In summer, the cobblestone streets are shaded by ancient trees, and the air is filled with the scent of jasmine and osmanthus. The Tea Horse Road section features old stone bridges and canals. Photograph the bridges from a low angle to emphasize their arches, or capture the reflections in the canals. The best time is late afternoon, when the long shadows create a dramatic interplay of light and dark.

The Sifang Street in Shuhe

Unlike the crowded Sifang Street in Lijiang, Shuhe’s version is peaceful and uncrowded. In summer, the square is surrounded by cafés with outdoor seating, and the sound of running water from a nearby creek adds to the ambiance. Photograph the square from a café balcony, using the overhanging vines as a natural frame. For a more intimate shot, focus on the details: a wooden door, a hanging lantern, or a cat sleeping on a stone step.

Baisha Village: The Mural Masterpieces

The Baisha Murals

Baisha Village, about 10 kilometers north of Lijiang, is known for its ancient murals that blend Tibetan, Naxi, and Han Chinese styles. The murals are housed in the Dabaoji Palace, a small temple. Summer light streams through the windows, illuminating the faded colors. Use a tripod and a low ISO to capture the details without flash, which can damage the paintings. The murals depict Buddhist deities and scenes from daily life—photograph sections that tell a story, such as a group of monks or a harvest scene.

The Old Market

Baisha’s old market operates every morning, but in summer, it’s less crowded. The market is held in a square shaded by a giant banyan tree. Photograph the vendors selling local produce, handmade silver jewelry, and dried mushrooms. Use a 35mm lens for street photography—capture the wrinkled hands of an elderly woman sorting herbs, or the smile of a child holding a sugar cane. The light is perfect between 8:00 AM and 10:00 AM, when the sun is low and the shadows are long.

Practical Tips for Summer Photography in Lijiang

Gear and Preparation

Summer in Lijiang is unpredictable—expect rain one moment and sunshine the next. Pack a weather-sealed camera body and lenses, or use a rain cover. A polarizing filter is essential for reducing glare on water and enhancing colors. A tripod is necessary for low-light shots, especially at Wangu Tower and Black Dragon Pool. Bring extra batteries and memory cards, as the humidity can drain batteries faster than usual.

Timing and Weather

The best light in summer is during the golden hours—6:00 AM to 8:00 AM and 5:00 PM to 7:00 PM. Midday light is harsh and flat, so use that time for indoor photography or rest. Check the weather forecast daily; summer storms often clear by late afternoon, leaving behind dramatic clouds and clear skies. If it rains, don’t give up—some of the best shots are taken during or immediately after a storm, when the air is clean and the colors are saturated.

Respecting Local Culture

Lijiang is home to the Naxi people, who have a rich cultural heritage. When photographing locals, especially in Baisha or Shuhe, ask for permission first. A smile and a nod often suffice. Avoid photographing religious ceremonies without consent, and never use flash in temples or murals. Be mindful of your surroundings—don’t step on crops or disturb wildlife in Lashi Lake.

Final Thoughts on Lijiang Summer Photography

Lijiang in summer is not just about iconic landmarks—it’s about capturing the essence of a living culture against a backdrop of natural beauty. The monsoon clouds, the blooming flowers, the soft light, and the warm hospitality of the Naxi people all contribute to a photographer’s dream. Whether you’re aiming for that perfect reflection at Black Dragon Pool or a candid portrait in Baisha, each spot offers a unique perspective. Remember to slow down, observe, and let the scene unfold naturally. The best photographs are often the ones you least expect—a sudden ray of light through the clouds, a child’s laughter echoing through the old streets, or the smell of jasmine after a summer rain. So pack your gear, embrace the unpredictability, and let Lijiang’s summer beauty guide your lens.
